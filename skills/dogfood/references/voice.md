# Voice and phone agents

The user is a caller who can't see anything, can't scroll back, and will talk over the agent. Little about this medium survives being judged from a transcript alone.

**Front door.** A real call on the real number through the real provider. A text harness against the same prompt tests the script rather than the experience, and the experience is where voice agents fail.

**Worth considering.** Interrupting mid-sentence, and whether it actually stops. Three seconds of silence. Background noise and a speakerphone. An accent, and a name it has to spell back. DTMF input. A transfer or handoff. A dropped or reconnected leg. A call long enough for the agent to lose the thread. Asking the same question twice. Something plainly outside its scope, to see how it declines. Callers invent situations no list predicts, so treat these as a warm-up rather than the whole test.

**Listen rather than read.** Latency, talking over the caller, dead air before a tool call, and a voice that sounds like it's reading a document are much of the point, and all four are invisible in a transcript. Play the audio.

**Hand over the audio.** Judging naturalness is a human call. Report the concrete failures you can prove (it didn't stop when interrupted, four seconds of silence after "let me check that") and hand over the recording rather than a verdict on how it sounded.

**Findings that often show up.** Barge-in that doesn't cut the audio, silence during a tool call with nothing said to fill it, written phrasing that lands badly out loud, numbers and dates read as raw digits, no recovery when it mishears a name.
